# The welfare ledger and the three rulers — the full working (text of manuscript v0.35, Ch 14 §5C, kept verbatim)

Replaced in v0.36 by two plain paragraphs. Numbers and their sources: `docs/THEOREMS_v1.md` (2026-11-10 entries), `code/results/joined_three_rulers.md`, `code/rig/hank_port/results_two_asset/TWO_ASSET_SWEEP_SUMMARY.md`.

The welfare ledger, read both ways. Every welfare ranking of
instruments in this section compares what the taxed economy gains
from a program with what the standard economy gains from the same
program; the difference is the mechanism's share. On that measure
stability beats the equal-dollar transfer in nearly every economy of
the first host and in seven of ten of the third. The printed constants
of the third host fell among the other three, and the sentence that
stood here on their strength — that with two assets cash buys more
welfare than stability — is withdrawn as a general claim.

The standard ruler's own verdict reads differently, and it is the
one the word welfare will be taken to mean. Scored as that ruler
scores — a lifetime of consumption, valued by a chooser assumed whole —
a low-skill household inside the taxed economy does better with the
cash than with the equal-cost stability program in 178 of the 182
economies, and better with cash than with the time transfer in all
but one. At the
printed constants of the first host the cash raises that household's
lifetime value by 1.38 and the stability program, charged at the same
price, lowers it by 0.07: it gains 0.14 from steadier weeks and loses
0.22 to the tax that pays for them. Priced in cash, halving the
low-skill group's schedule entropy is worth to its members what a
transfer of two thousandths of mean quarterly income is worth — about
half a percent of their own pay — and across the third host's
economies from under one thousandth to fifteen. The program was
priced at twenty. That price was a supposition: what a steady
schedule costs to provide has not been measured, and at a price under
its worth the ranking turns. The time transfer fares worse on this
ledger, and by construction — the household of this host draws
nothing from an hour itself, only from what the hour does to its
plan, while the hour is charged at the wage.

The worth itself is that ruler's answer, and it is small, for a
reason with an ancestor. A household's mistakes in a savings plan
cost it little in lifetime utility, because near the best plan the
hill is flat (Cochrane 1989). The ruler counts a consumption stream
and nothing else, and in this host bandwidth reaches it only through
the savings plan. What the earlier chapters say a readable, drained,
time-poor life loses — aim, the gate, the mistake budget — has no
entry there.

On the other ruler — the chooser's own capacity, whose stand-in in
this host is bandwidth — the same programs read in the opposite
order. At the printed constants of the first host the time transfer
lifts the low-skill group's bandwidth from 0.30 to 0.65 and stability
lifts it to 0.42; the cash, at either size, leaves it at 0.29 to
0.30. So the two rulers disagree, and the disagreement is the
finding: a program that more than doubles a group's capacity to aim
registers on the consumption ruler as a loss. The standard ruler
supposes that a chooser's capacity is worth exactly what it adds to
consumption; Chapter 4's two rulers were built to measure that
capacity before anything priced it. Which ruler is trusted is a
premise, of the same kind as the two printed below, and no model
supplies it. What is owed, and not in hand, is the rate of exchange
between the two. Until it is, this section supports neither the
sentence that the modeled household is better off with stability nor
the sentence that it is better off with cash; it supports the
sentence that the answer turns on a ruler the standard model does not
carry — and that is a second reason, beside the first, never to quote
this section for the size of anything.

A third ruler needs a wall taken down. The household of this host
fails alone: its mistakes cost nobody else, and that is why they cost
so little. Put the same households in Chapter 11's boats — nine drawn
from the economy's three skill groups, a reader's accuracy rising with
bandwidth, which is a supposition and is marked as one — and score the
same programs by the town's famine (`code/joined_three_rulers.py`). At
the middle version of the supposition the town starves in 10.7 seasons
of a hundred with no program, 0.2 with the time transfer, 4.3 with
stability, and 11.3 and 10.8 with the two sizes of cash. The order —
time, stability, nothing, cash — is the bandwidth ruler's order
carried through, and it holds in all nine versions of the supposition
tried; what the town adds is the size. A ledger that adds up boats one
at a time sees the low-skill quarter's lost accuracy as a few percent
of the mean catch — 3.6 percent, when their readings fall from 70 in
a hundred to 60 — where the town sees its famine rate more than
double. And no transfer of catch from one hold to another, of any
size, moves a boat onto the fish: on the survival ruler cash works
only through the one door it has into bandwidth, scarcity, and at the
printed constants that door is nearly shut. Three rulers, then, and
the standard one is alone in its verdict. It is also alone in
supposing that a household's errors stop at its own door.

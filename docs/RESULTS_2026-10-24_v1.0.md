## §1. REFUTATION R4 — Theorem 6's plateau edge

### What is printed (Ch 6 §3, PROVEN)
> *The plateau extends up to a critical amount of aim, c₀, set by the
> environment; beyond c₀, every additional unit of aim must be purchased
> with visibility.* … c₀ = 0.016 (s = 0.3), 0.052 (s = 0.5), 0.138 (s = 0.7).

### What is true
The plateau (δ_A = 1 with aim > 0) exists — unchanged. But it extends far
past c₀. **Explicit construction (exact):** when the environment is wrong
(F ≠ E), act on the future: A = F. When the environment is right (F = E),
scatter: a = e with probability q, each other action with probability
(1−q)/(n−1). Choosing q so that p(a|e) is exactly uniform gives

    q = 1 / (n(1−s) + s) = λ*      (the same λ* as Theorem 6)

and the policy has δ_A = 1 exactly with aim

    c₀′(n, s) = 1 − h_n( s + (1−s)/n ) / log₂ n ,

where h_n(m) is the entropy of a row with match probability m. In words:
**the hidden agent's aim at the plateau equals the aim of an agent who
follows the future exactly as often as the environment is wrong.**
Equivalently c₀′(s) = 1 − δ_sage(1−s): what the covert agent may aim for
at noise s is what the sage must surrender at noise 1−s.

| n | s | c₀ printed (T6 construction) | **c₀′ exact (this construction)** | numerical edge (SLSQP, seeded) | δ_sage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 0.3 | 0.0158 | **0.0970** | 0.0970 | 0.5384 |
| 6 | 0.5 | 0.0522 | **0.2467** | **0.2774** | 0.7533 |
| 6 | 0.7 | 0.1381 | **0.4616** | 0.4616 | 0.9030 |
| 4 | 0.5 | 0.0848 | **0.2256** | 0.2256 | 0.7744 |

The numerical column is a multi-start constrained maximization of
I(A;F) over all policies with uniform p(a|e). At (6, 0.5) it finds an
*asymmetric* policy — a per-circumstance relabeling of actions, a code —
beating the symmetric construction (0.2774 > 0.2467, feasible to 10⁻¹⁴,
δ_A = 1.000000). So c₀′ is an **exact lower bound on the plateau edge**;
the exact edge is **OPEN**, with a crude upper bound of 0.56 at (6, 0.5)
from the row-entropy constraint (every row with F = E is forced to have

## §2. THEOREM 19 — The economic hiding theorem (identification)

**Setting.** The book's model (Ch 4): future F, circumstances E (the
environment channel), action A. The econometrician — revealed
preference, random-utility estimation, any (price, quantity) record —
observes the joint law of **(A, E)** and nothing else: choice
probabilities p(a|e) and p(e). Call this *choice data*.

**Theorem 19.**
*(i) Independence is identified.* δ_A = H(A|E)/log₂|𝒜| is a function of
choice data alone.
*(ii) Aim is not.* For fixed choice data P, aim = I(A;F)/H(F) is
unidentified over an interval [aim_min(P), aim_max(P)], where aim_min is
the reading on which the residual randomness is noise (A independent of
F given E) and aim_max the reading on which it is payload (maximum
I(A;F) over all policies consistent with P).
*(iii) The interval is wide, by construction.* Two exact pairs:
  - **Nadia and Ines.** Uniform choice data (every circumstance, every
    action equally likely) is produced by pure scatter (aim 0) and by
    the plateau policy of §1 (aim c₀′ = 0.2467; numerically ≥ 0.2774).
  - **The sage and the half-autopilot town.** Choice data
    "follow the room with probability ½, scatter otherwise" is produced
    by a population of Tomas-and-Nadia at aim 0.069 — and, *identically*,
    by the sage (A = F seen through a 50%-reliable environment) at aim
    1.000. The perfectly competent agent and the near-aimless population
    leave the same fingerprint in every revealed-preference dataset.
*(iv) Welfare is blind by the same width.* Any such P with full support
is rationalized exactly by a logit random-utility model with utilities
u(a|e) = log p(a|e); its welfare (the logsum) is the same number at every
point of the interval. The destruction of aim is not a mis-estimate in
the random-utility model; it is unobservable in it.
*(v) The missing column.* Recording the realized future F beside the
action — panel data linking choices to outcomes — collapses the interval
to a point: aim becomes a direct computation.

**Proof sketches.** (i) immediate from the definition. (ii) The feasible
set {p(a|f,e) : Σ_f p(f|e) p(a|f,e) = p(a|e)} is a nonempty polytope
(the noise reading is a point of it); I(A;F) is continuous, so the image
is a closed interval; it is convex in the channel, so aim_max sits at a
vertex. (iii) The pairs are explicit; their p(a|e) agree by direct
computation (the sage's p(a|e) = p(f = a|e) is exactly the ½-mixture
row). (iv) With a fixed choice set and positive probabilities, logit with
u = log p reproduces p exactly; logsum depends on u alone. (v) I(A;F) is
a functional of the (A,F) law. ∎

**Tags.** (i), (iii), (iv), (v): PROVEN (constructive). The numerical
endpoints of (ii) at non-degenerate P: NUMERICAL, lower bounds.

**Computed table** (n = 6, s = 0.5; choice data = legible rule w.p. μ,
scatter w.p. 1−μ):

| μ | δ_A (identified) | aim_min (noise) | aim_max | exact witness for aim_max | width |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.00 | 0.000 | 0.247 | 0.247 | (no residual; pinned) | 0.000 |
| 0.90 | 0.235 | 0.203 | ≥ 0.322 | numerical | ≥ 0.12 |
| 0.75 | 0.473 | 0.146 | ≥ 0.515 | numerical | ≥ 0.37 |
| **0.50** | **0.753** | **0.069** | **1.000** | **the sage, exactly** | **0.931** |
| 0.25 | 0.931 | 0.019 | ≥ 0.527 | numerical | ≥ 0.51 |
| 0.10 | 0.988 | 0.003 | ≥ 0.346 | numerical | ≥ 0.34 |
| **0.00** | **1.000** | **0.000** | **≥ 0.277** | **c₀′ = 0.247 exact; 0.277 numerical** | **≥ 0.28** |

Reading: at every level of visible randomness, the data are consistent
with aim anywhere across most of its range. The logsum column is 0.000
on every row.

**The congruence, stated carefully.** This is the same shape as the hiding
theorem of arXiv:2608.18886: a physical quantity (there: the cos 4(a−b)
fringe; here: aim) that vanishes *identically* from the canonical data
(there: the four CHSH angles; here: the (A,E) law), not approximately but
term by term, together with the instrument that sees it (there: the
settings torus; here: the (A,F) column). Same bookkeeping. Printed as a
resonance, not a premise — the theorem stands on its own arithmetic.

**Field consequence.** Datasets that record actions *and* later outcomes
for the same person (longitudinal panels) are the economic torus scan.
Datasets that record choices and prices only are structurally blind to
the quantity, however large.

## §3. PROPOSITION 13.1 — Goodhart immunity of the two-ruler ledger

**Setting.** A ledger scores a population on (aim, δ_A) with F recorded
(the missing column present). A party that controls the population's
actions may post-process them using anything it sees — the actions, the
circumstances, private noise — but not the future: A′ = h(A, E, N), with
N independent of F given (A, E).

**Proposition.** *(i) No manufacture of aim.* I(A′;F) ≤ I(A,E;F): aim
cannot be raised above what the workers and the room already jointly
know. *(ii) No free independence.* (A′, E) is itself a feasible policy,
so its point lies on or inside the frontier: δ_A cannot be raised at a
better exchange rate than the frontier's. *(iii) Pure noise is detectable.*
Injecting noise independent of E traces the mixing family, which lies
strictly inside the frontier on this class (the mixing-sage refutation;
now known to be far inside near δ_A = 1 by §1). A population moved by
noise injection sits visibly off-frontier. *(iv) Consequence.* No
manipulation without future-information produces a Pareto improvement on
the plane: the two rulers can be traded against each other only at a
rate no better than the honest one, and never both raised. Gaming
independence crashes aim; gaming aim (coercion toward the room) crashes
independence — and both show on the ledger as what they are.

**Proof.** (i) Data-processing: F → (A,E) → A′ is a Markov chain by the
hypothesis on N. (ii) Definition of the frontier as the maximum aim at
each δ_A over all policies. (iii) The mixing family's curve is the
refuted candidate; the frontier dominates it. (iv) is (i)+(ii). ∎

**Tags.** (i), (ii), (iv): PROVEN. (iii): EXACT on class (numbers carry
passports).

**The attack surface that remains — printed at full strength.** The
immunity is against manipulating *actions*. It is not immunity against
manipulating *the ledger's definitions*. If the audited party is allowed
to define F (the "future" against which aim is scored — say, its own
KPIs) or E (which circumstances count as the room), aim is measured
against a chosen target, and Proposition 13.1 does not apply. The
defense is procedural, not mathematical: **F must be an outcome the
audited party does not choose, recorded by someone it does not pay.**
That is the one place Goodhart still lives, and the ledger should say so.

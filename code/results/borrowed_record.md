# The borrowed record (2026-11-12) — a voice that is right where the answer is easy and wrong where it is hard

Printed town: 30% of days are hard. Readers: right 90% of plain days, 55% of hard days. The sage: right on every plain day, 50% of hard days — worse than any single reader exactly when the day is hard.

| | right, all days (the record) | right on plain days | right on hard days |
|---|---|---|---|
| one reader | 79.5% | 90% | 55% |
| the readers' majority | 88.6% | 99.91% | 62.1% |
| the sage | 85.0% | 100% | 50% |

One tally per voice: the sage's record (85.0%) beats every reader's (79.5%), so every fisher follows him — correctly, on the tally he keeps.

| arrangement | bad days | famine (three running in ninety) |
|---|---|---|
| everyone reads | 11.4% | 11.0% |
| everyone follows the sage | 15.0% | 22.5% |
| two tallies: follow him on plain days, read on hard ones | 11.4% | 10.9% |

Followers by number (one tally): famine 0: 11.0%, 1: 12.0%, 2: 14.2%, 3: 17.5%, 4: 20.8%, 5: 22.5%, 6: 22.5%, 7: 22.5%, 8: 22.5%, 9: 22.5%.

Where the record was earned: on plain days the majority was already right 99.91% of the time. Everything the sage is right about, the town already knew. Every day on which following him differs from reading is a hard day, and on those he is right 50% of the time against the majority's 62.1%.

## How long the standing lasts

A hard day's outcome is recorded against the voice that called it with probability v.

| share of hard-day outcomes recorded (v) | believed never wrong: days to the first recorded miss | believed right 95% on hard days, at odds 99 to 1: days until the odds turn |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7 (0.1 seasons) | 18 (0.2 seasons) |
| 0.3 | 22 (0.2 seasons) | 61 (0.7 seasons) |
| 0.1 | 67 (0.7 seasons) | 184 (2.0 seasons) |
| 0.03 | 222 (2.5 seasons) | 615 (6.8 seasons) |
| 0.01 | 667 (7.4 seasons) | 1,845 (20.5 seasons) |

Standing lasts in inverse proportion to how much of the hard part is recorded. Record all of it and the borrowed record is gone inside a month; record one hard day in a hundred and it outlasts seven seasons of famine at 23% each.

## How wide the condition is

Grid: hard-day share h ∈ {0.1 … 0.5}; readers p_e ∈ {0.8, 0.9, 0.95}, p_h ∈ {0.4 … 0.65}; sage right on every plain day, q_h ∈ {0.25 … 0.6}.

- the sage's single tally beats every reader's AND following him raises the town's bad days: 185 of 450 worlds;
- his single tally beats every reader's while he is worse than each single reader on hard days: 195 of 450;
- both at once: 160 of 450.

## The gate beside him (Chapter 5's terms)
A neighbor who cannot tell a plain day from a hard one needs knowledge better than the sage's RECORD (85%) before acting on it is rational — not better than his accuracy on the days that decide anything (50%). The gate is set by the record, and the record was set by the easy days.

## Reading (first row: the printed town's 70% reader, split into clear and murky mornings)
- With sailing cheap, every boat goes and the town is Chapter 11's: famine 7.4%.
- Let a trip cost more than a murky reading is worth (0.4 of a boatload) and four boats in ten stay in on any morning. Each is right to: its own net catch rises (third column). The town's famine goes from 7.4% to 98.2%.
- The murky boat was carrying the town: it landed a boatload four mornings in ten, and the town's good day is a count of boatloads, not of confident fishers.
- The remedy is small and exact: cover the gap between the cost of a doubtful trip and what the doubtful reading is worth. At a cost five hundredths of a boatload above the murky reading's worth, that is 2.9% of the mean catch — against a famine rate cut from 98% to 7%.
- This is Theorem 5b in a boat. A wrong bet of one's own that costs more than it is worth is not made; a town that prices its fishers' doubtful mornings at their private worth loses the boats it was living on.

## Reading
- Two towns of readers: bad days fall from 9.9% to 3.47%, famine from 7.4% to 0.354%. A reading town fails by a boat or two, and a neighbor's surplus covers a boat or two.
- Two towns each wholly behind its own master: aid buys nothing. A herd fails all at once — no boats on the fish — and a neighbor's whole surplus (four boatloads) cannot replace five.
- Two towns behind one master fail on the same days; whatever aid could have bought, there is no surplus on the day it is needed.
- So a transfer is worth exactly what the sameness between giver and receiver leaves it: inside one town on a bad day, nothing (the transfer lemma); between towns whose errors are their own, almost everything; between towns that follow the same voice, nothing again.
